## 2. ABOUT (the long block on the profile page)

```
PERM Tracker follows the PERM labor certification process using the Department of Labor's own published data.

Look up any case number and see where it stands, how far DOL has worked into the queue, and how long cases filed around the same time have taken. Every deadline is computed from your case dates: the filing window, the prevailing wage expiration, and the audit and appeal response dates under 20 CFR 656. Looking up a case is free and needs no account.

Two kinds of people use it, and it is built for both. If you are waiting on your own case, it tells you where you are and what happens next. If you file for others, it tracks deadlines across a whole caseload with calendar and timeline views.

We are practitioners on both sides of this: attorneys who file, beneficiaries who have waited, and the engineers who built the thing. That is why it does not read like software written for only one of them.

We write about PERM processing times, what the queue data actually shows, audits and RFIs, and how the deadlines work. Everything is sourced to DOL's own figures or to the regulation it comes from, and dated.

permtracker.app
```

## What changed, and why

**The old copy said "built specifically for immigration attorneys."** That is the
single line most worth removing. The site was restructured away from it in
August because heading structure is what answer engines aggregate into "what
this product is", and that framing is precisely why AI overviews were
describing PERM Tracker as attorney-only software. Medium is a high-authority
domain that those same systems read, so leaving it there works against the fix.

It is also wrong on the traffic. Measured in Search Console over 90 days,
"perm tracker" brought **1,088 of 2,300 clicks** — roughly half the site's
search traffic, and overwhelmingly people looking up their own case. The old
bio spoke to none of them.

**"Built by practitioners, for practitioners" stays**, now that you have
confirmed the team includes attorneys and beneficiaries. It is the most
testable claim on the profile, which is exactly why it needed confirming
before it shipped — and it is a genuinely strong one, so the About says who
"practitioners" means instead of leaving it as a slogan.

**Two house-style fixes.** The old About had an em-dash ("the actual
regulations — not vague advice") and the banned "X, not Y" construction in the
same clause. Both are gone; the replacement states the sourcing rule directly.

**One thing I did not change.** The profile lists pronouns as "he/him" on what
is a company account. Harmless, and yours to decide, but Medium shows it next
to the name and it reads oddly for a brand.
