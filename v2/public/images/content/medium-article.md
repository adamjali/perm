# PERM in 2026: 7 Costly Mistakes That Add Months to Your Timeline

If you're an immigration attorney managing PERM cases in 2026, every mistake compounds. One wrong date cascades through 15+ downstream deadlines. One inconsistent job posting triggers an audit with a 40% denial rate. One missed filing window means starting over from scratch.

I've seen the same seven mistakes trip up attorneys year after year — from solo practitioners to large firms. Here's what they are, why they happen, and exactly how to avoid them.

## Mistake 1: Treating the PWD as "Just Paperwork"

The prevailing wage determination isn't administrative busywork. It's the foundation every other deadline is built on.

Under [20 CFR 656.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-D/section-656.40), the PWD expiration date dictates your recruitment window, your ETA 9089 filing window, and ultimately whether your case survives DOL scrutiny. The [National Prevailing Wage Center](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/wages) is currently processing requests filed 6-8 months ago — meaning if you filed a PWD today, you're looking at a determination date in late 2026.

**The fix:** File the PWD the moment you have a finalized job description and SOC code. Don't wait for the client to "finalize" the position. Use [O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/) to validate your SOC code selection — the wrong code produces a different prevailing wage, which cascades into every downstream decision.

For a deep dive into PWD strategy and SOC code selection, see this [complete PERM filing guide](https://permtracker.app/guides/complete-perm-filing-guide).

## Mistake 2: Ignoring the Cascade Effect

Here's what most attorneys miss: PERM deadlines aren't independent. They form a chain of dependencies that cascade forward.

Change the PWD determination date? Your PWD expiration shifts. That shifts your recruitment window close date. That shifts your filing window. That shifts your I-140 deadline.

One date change touches 10+ downstream deadlines. And most attorneys are tracking this on spreadsheets — manually recalculating every dependent date when something changes.

## Mistake 3: Getting Recruitment Sequencing Wrong

This is the #1 audit trigger in 2026, and the DOL's [30% integrity review target](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/stricter-scrutiny-of-labor-certifications-increase-in-audits-and-supervised-recruitment-by-the-dol-2/) means more cases are getting scrutinized than ever.

Under [20 CFR 656.17](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.17), recruitment activities must follow a specific sequence:

- Job order must be placed before other recruitment methods begin
- Newspaper ads must run on Sundays
- The second Sunday ad must follow the first
- For professional occupations, additional recruitment steps apply
- The notice of filing must be posted for 10 consecutive business days

Get the sequence wrong, and the DOL doesn't give you a chance to fix it. They audit — and audited cases have a 40% denial rate with 224 days of additional processing time.

**The fix:** Use a recruitment compliance checklist that validates timing and sequencing, not just completion. ## Mistake 4: Missing the 30-180 Day Filing Window

The ETA 9089 filing window is one of the most unforgiving deadlines in PERM. You can't file until 30 days after your last recruitment activity ends. But you must file before 180 days after your first recruitment activity started — or before your PWD expires, whichever comes first.

Miss the opening? You wait. Miss the close? You restart. The window is narrower than most attorneys realize, especially when PWD processing delays push everything back.

The DOL's [FLAG system](https://flag.dol.gov/programs/perm) doesn't warn you when your window is closing. The October 2025 government shutdown proved how fragile the system is — FLAG went offline for 30 days, and the [DOL granted only 33 days of grace](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dol-announces-accommodations-for-perm-and-other-filings-impacted-by-the-government-shutdown.html) for expired deadlines.

**The fix:** Track your filing window status actively — Open Soon, Open, Closing Soon, Closed, Expired. Tools like [PERM Tracker](https://permtracker.app) calculate this automatically from your recruitment dates and PWD expiration.

For the full regulatory breakdown, see the [filing window section](https://permtracker.app/guides/ultimate-perm-guide-2026) in our strategic guide.

## Mistake 5: Underestimating the Remote Work Problem

Remote and hybrid positions have created a new category of PERM pitfalls. The PWD, recruitment ads, notice of filing, and ETA 9089 must all tell a consistent location story.

According to [Arvian Immigration's analysis](https://arvian-immigration.com/perm-for-remote-hybrid-multi-location-in-2025-2026-how-to-align-worksite-pwd-nof-ads-and-eta-9089/), the most common mistakes include:

- Using "remote" language that implies "anywhere in the US" when the PWD is tied to a specific geographic area
- Inconsistent location descriptions between recruitment ads and the ETA 9089
- Selecting the wrong prevailing wage area for hybrid positions

The DOL doesn't have clear-cut rules for remote PERM filings — which means they have maximum discretion to audit cases that look inconsistent.

**The fix:** Define the primary worksite before you file the PWD. Use the exact same geographic description in every document. If the position is hybrid, document the hybrid schedule consistently. And never use the word "remote" without specifying the actual work location.

## Mistake 6: Not Preparing for the RFI Shift

In 2025, the DOL quietly shifted from formal audits to Requests for Information (RFIs). According to [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw-insidebusinessimmigration.com/perm/dol-expands-use-of-rfis-in-perm-cases/), RFIs are now the DOL's preferred tool for resolving case issues — and unlike formal audits, they're not even defined in the PERM regulations.

The response window is the same — 30 days — but the consequences of missing it are identical to a missed audit response: denial.

Here's the problem: most attorneys aren't set up to track RFI deadlines separately from audit deadlines. An RFI arrives by email, gets buried in an inbox, and by the time someone notices, the 30-day clock has nearly run out.

**The fix:** Treat RFIs with the same urgency as audits. Set up a separate tracking system for RFI received dates and response deadlines. [PERM Tracker](https://permtracker.app) automatically calculates RFI due dates (+30 days from receipt) and sends push notifications as the deadline approaches.

## Mistake 7: Treating the I-140 as an Afterthought

The I-140 filing deadline is 180 days from the ETA 9089 certification date. It sounds generous — until you factor in the current processing landscape.

With [PERM analyst review processing August 2024 filings](https://www.envoyglobal.com/insight/perm-processing-updates-issued-by-dol/) as of January 2026, by the time your case gets certified, your client's circumstances may have changed. New employer, new location, new job duties — any of which could require a new PERM case entirely.

And with EB-2 India facing severe visa bulletin retrogression and even [EB-1 India retrogressing to March 2022](https://manifestlaw.com/blog/eb1-priority-date-india/), the priority date established by your PERM filing is more valuable than ever. Missing the I-140 window doesn't just lose the case — it loses years of visa queue position.

**The fix:** Calendar the I-140 deadline the moment you receive ETA 9089 certification. Begin I-140 preparation during the PERM processing period, not after. For the regulatory requirements, [USCIS I-140 guidance](https://www.uscis.gov/i-140) covers the filing specifics.
