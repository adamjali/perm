**What you'll learn:** how to use PERM Tracker day to day. Adding cases, reading the dashboard, working a case from its own page, recruitment, RFIs and denials, the calendar, reminders and the assistant.

## Who this is for

PERM Tracker is free, and it has two halves. Public pages for anyone waiting on a [PERM labor certification](https://flag.dol.gov/programs/perm), and a case manager for the people filing them:

- **The person waiting on a case**: look your own case number up at [PERM case status](/perm-case-status), get an email when DOL changes it, and read the same federal data everyone else is reading. The same lookup and the same alerts take prevailing wage requests (P-) and H-1B labor condition applications (I-). No account, no sign-in.
- **Immigration attorneys** managing a caseload of PERM cases
- **Paralegals** tracking deadlines and recruitment
- **HR teams and employers** keeping track of their own cases

No credit card. No trial period. Free forever.

Everything below this point covers the signed-in case manager. If you're waiting on your own case and don't want an account, [check the case status](/perm-case-status) and [read the PERM data](/guides/reading-the-perm-data) instead.

## The PERM process in 60 seconds

Every PERM case follows four stages. PERM Tracker tracks them all:

1. **PWD**: file a prevailing wage determination request with DOL. The determination sets the wage and has an expiration date.
2. **Recruitment**: run the required recruitment: the job order, two Sunday ads, the notice of filing, and three more steps for a professional job.
3. **ETA 9089**: file the PERM application once the 30-day quiet period after recruitment has passed, then wait for certification (or an RFI, an audit or a denial).
4. **I-140**: file the immigrant petition within 180 days of certification. DOL prints the last day, certification plus 179, and the app uses it.

Each stage has deadlines, and missing one can mean starting over. That's what PERM Tracker is for.

## The dashboard

After you sign in, you land on the **Dashboard**:

- **DOL's queue**: one line saying which filing month DOL is deciding PERM cases from.
- **Deadlines**: every deadline across your cases in four groups: overdue, this week, this month and later. A deadline moves between the groups on its own as its day comes closer.
- **Cases**: how many cases sit at each stage, drawn as one bar. Each count opens the cases page filtered to that stage. A **Duplicates** count appears when two cases look like the same filing.
- **Recent changes**: the latest updates across your cases, each with its job and stage.

A new account also sees a short setup checklist, and a banner appears when auto-close has closed a case (see [Notifications](#notifications)).

## Cases

The **Cases** page shows every case as a card, or as rows in a list. Switch between them with the two buttons beside the heading.

- **Card view**: each card names the employer and the job, shows the stage on its tab, and leads with the next deadline and the days left. A small label under it says where the work stands (working on it, filed, RFI open). Open **Dates** on a card to see all its dates; it stays open until you close it.
- **Active, Completed, Closed, All**: the tabs above the search.
- **Search**: finds a case by employer, foreign worker ID, job, notes or any case number, and forgives a typo.
- **Filters**: stage, progress and duplicates only.
- **Sort**: next deadline, recently updated, favorites first, employer name, case status, the date the PWD, ETA 9089 or I-140 was filed, or your own order by dragging.
- **Favorites**: star a case to keep it near the top.
- **Select**: pick several cases to export them (CSV or JSON), archive them, reopen closed ones, sync them to Google Calendar or delete them.
- **Import / export**: bring a caseload in from a JSON file, or export the cases the list shows.

Press **⌘K** (Ctrl+K on Windows) anywhere in the app to search your own cases by name.

## Adding and editing a case

Click **Add case** on the dashboard or the cases page. The form has these parts:

- **Basic information**: the employer, the job, the foreign worker ID, the DOL case number and your own reference. The stage follows the dates you enter unless you switch that off and set it yourself.
- **PWD**, **Recruitment**, **ETA 9089** and **I-140**: one section for each stage's dates.
- **Job description**: the duties and requirements, with templates you can save and reuse.
- **Notes and settings**: Google Calendar sync, favorite, and whether the case shows on the timeline.

<Callout type="info" title="Save at any time">
  You don't have to fill everything in at once. Enter what you have, save, and come back later.
</Callout>

### How the sections work

- **A checkmark** shows when a section is complete, with a one-line summary (for example "Determined Jun 15 · Expires Dec 31 · $95,000 LII").
- **The next section opens on its own** when you finish the one before it, such as entering the PWD determination date.
- **Any section opens at any time**, even before its stage. A note says what usually comes first.
- **Dates work themselves out**: the PWD expiration, the recruitment deadlines, the filing window and the I-140 deadline. These carry an "Auto" tag.

### Checks as you type

- **Red asterisks (*)** mark required fields.
- **Amber warnings** flag something worth a second look but don't stop you saving.
- **Impossible dates** are refused, such as a certification before the filing.

### Starting from the deadline calculator

Worked a case out on the public [PERM deadline calculator](/tools/perm-deadline-calculator)? Choose **Save as a case** there and the dates come with you into a new case, after you sign in or create an account.

### The top of the page

- **The case's name, stage and progress**, with a **Professional** label when the extra recruitment steps apply, and a bar across the four stages.
- **The DOL case number**, once you've entered it, links to its live status on the public [case lookup](/perm-case-status).
- **Buttons**: star the case, turn its Google Calendar sync on or off, **Edit case**, and a menu to show it on the timeline or take it off, reopen, archive or delete it. Archiving closes a case; it doesn't delete it.

### The tabs

- **Overview**: **Next up**, the one thing to do next, where you can record the date it happened without opening the form. Below it: the prevailing wage (case number, wage level and amount, expiration), quick stats (days with DOL, deadlines due in the next 30 days), every step so far on one line of milestones, and the job description. Once recruitment is complete, a **recruitment results** summary is written for you to edit and copy.
- **Recruitment**: the recruitment window, then every step with its dates: the job order, the Sunday ads, the notice of filing and any additional steps.
- **ETA 9089**: the filing window, the filing, any audit, the certification and its expiration, any RFIs, and a denial with the review that follows it.
- **I-140**: the I-140 filing window, the filing and receipt, the category, premium processing, the service center, the outcome and any RFEs.
- **Documents**: upload, preview and sort the case's files by kind.
- **Notes**: the case's journal. Each note can be marked done once it's handled.

## RFIs, RFEs and denials

The app tracks a deadline for each: an RFI from DOL, an RFE from USCIS and a denied ETA 9089. DOL's two give 30 days, worked out for you, and an RFE's due date comes from the notice.

### An RFI from DOL

An RFI (request for information) comes while DOL reviews the ETA 9089.

- **30 days to answer**: enter the date it came and the due date is worked out and locked.
- Record when you answered, and the deadline leaves your list.
- The case card and the dashboard show it as it comes closer.

<Callout type="warning" title="The RFI deadline">
  DOL's 30 days don't stretch. Enter the date the RFI came the day it arrives, so the deadline is on your dashboard and in your reminders from the start.
</Callout>

### An RFE from USCIS

An RFE (request for evidence) comes while USCIS reviews the I-140.

- **You enter the due date** from the notice: typically 30 to 90 days, 87 as standard.
- Record when you answered, as with an RFI.

### A denied ETA 9089

Enter the date on DOL's denial letter in the ETA 9089 section, and the case shows the 30 days you have to ask for reconsideration by the Certifying Officer or review by BALCA. If you ask for reconsideration and it's upheld, enter the date of that notice and the case shows the 30 days to ask BALCA. The deadline reaches the dashboard, the calendar and your reminders like any other. A certification entered after a denial counts as a reversal.

| | RFI | RFE | Denial review |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage** | ETA 9089 | I-140 | ETA 9089 |
| **From** | DOL | USCIS | DOL |
| **Deadline** | 30 days, worked out | From the notice, entered by you | 30 days from the letter, worked out |

## Professional occupations and additional recruitment

For a job that needs a bachelor's degree or higher, the regulations ([20 CFR 656.17(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.17)) require **3 additional recruitment steps** beyond the standard ones.

### Turning it on

Switch on **Professional occupation** in the Recruitment section when the job needs a bachelor's degree or higher.

### The 11 steps to choose from

Local or ethnic newspaper ad, radio ad, TV ad, job fair, campus placement office, trade or professional organization, private employment firm, employee referral program, the employer's website, a job website, and on-campus recruitment.

### How each step is dated

- **A start and end date**: a job website ad, the employer's website, a private employment firm.
- **Several dated entries**: radio and TV ads, one for each time it ran.
- **One date**: every other step.

### Common mistakes

- **Two steps**: you need three. Recruitment isn't complete until three steps have dates.
- **A step with no date** doesn't count.
- **A step dated before the wage determination**, or after the last day the steps can run, is flagged.

<Callout type="important" title="Three extra steps are required">
  A professional job needs at least 3 additional recruitment steps, each with its dates. Two isn't enough.
</Callout>

## Calendar and timeline

The calendar shows every deadline across your cases by month, week or day, and can sync to Google Calendar. The timeline draws each case's stages as bars, every case in one view.

### The calendar

Every deadline across your cases, by month, week or day:

- **Coloured by stage** (PWD, recruitment, ETA 9089, I-140), with filters for what to show.
- **Point at an entry** for a summary, or **click it** for the details and a link to the case.

### Google Calendar sync

1. Go to **Settings > Calendar sync**.
2. Click **Connect calendar**.
3. Choose which kinds of deadline to send.

Every case syncs once you connect. To leave one case out, switch its sync off on its page or in its **Notes and settings**. **Sync all cases** in Settings, and **Select** on the cases page, change many at once.

### The timeline

Every case on one timeline, each stage drawn as a bar, so you can see which cases run side by side and where the deadlines bunch up.

## Notifications

Settings control which emails come, browser notifications and quiet hours, plus auto-close, which is off to start with.

### Email

Under **Settings > Notifications**:

- **Deadline reminders** by stage: PWD, recruitment, ETA 9089, I-140, RFI responses and RFE responses. They arrive gathered into one email a day.
- **When to remind you**: 30, 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before a deadline, all on to start with.
- **Case updates**, and a **weekly summary** of the whole caseload on Mondays, sent only in a week when something changed.

### In the browser

Turn on browser notifications in the same tab and allow them when your browser asks. They reach you without the app open.

### Quiet hours

**Settings > Quiet hours** holds notifications during the hours you choose.

### Auto-close

**Settings > Auto-close** is off to start with. Turn it on and a case closes itself when a deadline that can't be met has passed: a PWD that expired before the ETA 9089 was filed (unless recruitment began while it was valid), or a filing window that closed. You get a notification and a banner on the dashboard, and the case stays readable.

## The assistant

The chat button in the corner opens an assistant that knows PERM and can read your cases.

### What it can do

- **Answer PERM questions**: the rules, the timelines, what comes next.
- **Read your cases**: "Which cases have deadlines this week?" or "What's left on the Juniper case?"
- **Check a deadline**: "When does the filing window close for this case?"
- **Make changes for you**: update a date, change a stage, add a note. It shows the change before it runs, and checks it against the PERM rules first.
- **Search the web** for current news, such as a rule change.

### Three modes

Choose at the top of the chat panel:

- **Read only**: answers questions and changes nothing.
- **Ask first** (the default): proposes each change and waits for your yes.
- **Auto**: makes changes straight away. Deleting still asks.

## Tips and common pitfalls

Enter dates as you get them, starting with the PWD determination. The common pitfalls are too few professional steps, an RFI date entered late, recruitment begun after the PWD expires and a forgotten notice of filing.

### Good habits

- **Enter dates as you get them**. You don't need the whole case at once.
- **Start with the PWD determination**: the expiration and the recruitment deadlines hang on it.
- **Let the app work out the quiet period**: the filing window opens 30 days after the last recruitment step, and closes 180 days after the first.
- **The sample case** a new account starts with is marked **Sample**, sends you nothing, and can be deleted any time.

### Pitfalls

- **Professional jobs need 3 additional steps**: not 2, not 1. Three, each with its dates.
- **An RFI gives 30 days**: enter the date it came straight away.
- **Recruitment has to begin before the PWD expires**: once it has begun inside the PWD's validity, the window closes 180 days from the first recruitment step even if the PWD expires first. Recruitment begun before the determination date has to be filed by the PWD expiration.
- **Don't forget the notice of filing**: 10 business days of posting, and easy to overlook.
