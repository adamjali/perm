This guide is for someone whose PERM is still waiting at DOL while the visa bulletin already shows their priority date as current. It happens when a category's cutoff reaches your PERM's filing date before DOL's queue does. It's good news with a catch: nothing can be filed with USCIS until DOL certifies the PERM.

<Callout type="info" title="Why the date is already yours">
Your priority date is "the date the labor certification application was accepted for processing by any office of the Department of Labor" (8 CFR 204.5(d)). That's your PERM's filing date. It's fixed now, which is why you can already compare it to the bulletin, even though the petition that will carry it hasn't been filed.
</Callout>

## What can be filed while the PERM waits

The I-140, the I-485, and the EAD and advance parole all wait for the certification. An H-1B extension past six years can go in now if the PERM was filed 365 days before the extension would start.

## What happens on the day it's certified

The I-140 can be filed, the I-485 can go in with it if your date is still current, and the work permit and travel document follow.

<StepByStep>
  <Step number={1} title="The I-140 can be filed">
    The certification's 180-day clock starts too (20 CFR 656.30(b)(1)), so this is the moment the attorney has been waiting for. Premium processing guarantees USCIS action within "15 business days for most classifications".
  </Step>
  <Step number={2} title="If the date is still current, the I-485 can go in with it">
    Filing the two together, concurrently, is allowed when approving the I-140 "would make a visa immediately available" (8 CFR 245.2(a)(2)(i)(B)). USCIS names the chart that decides it each month. For October 2026 it was the Dates for Filing chart for employment-based cases.
  </Step>
  <Step number={3} title="The work permit and travel document follow">
    The I-765 and I-131 can be filed with the I-485 or after it, and renewals no longer extend automatically, as the [EAD guide](/guides/ead-renewal-no-automatic-extension) explains.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

## The risk while you wait

A current date can stop being current. The bulletin moves back when demand outruns the year's visas, and a date that's current in the month DOL certifies your case is the one that matters. If it moves back before the I-485 goes in, the I-140 can still be filed alone, and the I-485 waits for the next time the date is current. If it moves back after the I-485 is filed, the [retrogression guide](/guides/priority-date-retrogressed-after-filing) explains what stays in place.

Two things help:

- **An alert on the bulletin.** The [visa bulletin page](/visa-bulletin) emails you the day each new bulletin is published.
- **An alert on your case.** Your case page takes an email and writes when DOL changes the status, so you hear of the certification the same day.

## Get ready now

Gather the documents now: your PERM details, birth and marriage certificates, passports and visa records, degrees and transcripts, and experience letters. Time the medical exam with the attorney.

<PrintableChecklist title="Ready for the I-140 and I-485">

- [ ] Your PERM case number and filing date
- [ ] Birth certificate for you and each family member, with translations
- [ ] Passports, I-94 records and every visa you've held
- [ ] Degree certificates and transcripts the job's requirements rely on
- [ ] Experience letters matching the experience on the PERM
- [ ] Marriage certificate, if your spouse is in the case
- [ ] The medical exam, timed with the attorney (the [I-693 guide](/guides/i693-medical-exam) explains when)

</PrintableChecklist>

The [document checklist](/guides/perm-document-checklist) goes through each stage in more detail.

## What to ask the attorney

Ask whether the I-485 goes in with the I-140, whether the I-140 goes premium, which chart you'll check, and whether your family's documents can be reviewed now.

1. Is the I-485 going in with the I-140, or after it?
2. Will the I-140 go premium?
3. Which chart will we check, and on what date, before filing?
4. Can my family's documents be reviewed now, so nothing waits on them later?

Nothing here is legal advice. The attorney decides the filing plan and its timing.

## What this guide can't tell you

It can't tell you when DOL will certify your case, whether your date will still be current then, or which chart USCIS will name next month.

- **When DOL will certify your case.** The estimate is an estimate, and the scorecard shows its record.
- **Whether your date will still be current then.** The bulletin can move either way, and nobody can promise it won't move back.
- **Which chart USCIS will name next month.** It decides each month, usually within a week of the bulletin's publication.
