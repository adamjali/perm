This guide is for anyone whose sponsoring employer has announced layoffs, whether or not they're among those let go. A layoff touches a green card case at different points, and a different rule applies at each. Find your stage in the table, then read the section that goes with it.

## Before the PERM is filed: the six-month look-back

Under 20 CFR 656.17(k), if the employer has had a layoff in the area of intended employment within six months of filing, in the occupation the PERM is for or a related one, it has to document that it notified and considered all potentially qualified laid-off U.S. workers for the job. The regulation defines a layoff as "any involuntary separation of one or more employees without cause or prejudice." The employer attests to this on the form, and an audit asks for the documentation.

So a layoff in the same occupation and area inside that window adds a recruitment obligation the employer has to be able to prove. It doesn't bar the filing. A layoff outside the window, or in an unrelated occupation, is outside the rule. Whether yours falls inside it turns on dates, location and job title, which the attorney can check and this site can't.

## A PERM that's already filed

DOL decides the application it received. A layoff after filing doesn't change the attestations the employer made, and a pending PERM isn't pulled from the queue because the employer cut staff elsewhere. What can change is the employer's willingness to keep the job offer open, and a PERM is an offer of a job: if the position is eliminated, the employer withdraws the case, and WITHDRAWN is the status it shows.

If the employer is still sponsoring you, the case's place in line is unaffected. The [queue page](/perm-queue) shows where the filing month sits, and [case status](/perm-case-status) shows your own case.

## An approved I-140: the 180-day line

Once the I-140 has been approved for 180 days, an employer's withdrawal, or the employer going out of business, no longer revokes it automatically. Inside 180 days it does. That's 8 CFR 205.1(a)(3)(iii)(C) and (D), and it matters for two things: using the petition for an H-1B extension beyond six years, and using it as the basis of a pending I-485.

The priority date is a separate question and a better one. Under 8 CFR 204.5(e)(1) the date from an approved I-140 is kept for a later EB-1, EB-2 or EB-3 petition unless USCIS revoked the approval for fraud, willful misrepresentation, material error or an invalidated labor certification. An employer's withdrawal isn't on that list, so a new sponsor's PERM and I-140 can carry the old date. The [retention calculator](/tools/priority-date-retention) puts your dates on it, and the [revocation guide](/guides/i140-revoked-before-after-180-days) goes through each case.

## A pending I-485: portability at 180 days

If an I-485 has been pending 180 days, INA 204(j) and 8 CFR 245.25 let it be approved on a new offer in the same or a similar occupation, filed on Supplement J. USCIS decides whether the new job is same or similar, on its duties. Before 180 days, a withdrawal of the I-140 takes the I-485's basis with it.

## Losing the job itself: the H-1B grace period

8 CFR 214.1(l)(2) gives an H-1B worker whose employment ends up to 60 consecutive days, or until the end of the authorized validity period, whichever is shorter, in which to find a new petitioner, change status or leave. A new employer's H-1B petition filed inside that window keeps the status. A PERM by itself doesn't, because a PERM isn't a status.

The [H-1B six-year limit calculator](/tools/h1b-six-year-limit) shows whether a PERM or I-140 on file keeps the one-year extensions available, and the [six-year guide](/guides/h1b-six-year-limit-with-perm-pending) works through the dates.

## What the record can show you

- **The sponsor's PERM filings.** Its [employer page](/perm-employers) shows every PERM it has filed, decided and pending, by month, so a filing freeze shows as a gap.
- **Layoff notices.** The [layoff notices page](/layoffs) lists the WARN Act filings that 47 states and DC publish and ties each to the sponsor's record. Arkansas, New Hampshire and Wyoming publish no list, so a sponsor with no notice there isn't cleared. A state's WARN filing is the only public trace of a mass layoff, since DOL's PERM files record none.
- **Holds and appeals.** The [employers under review page](/perm-employers/under-review) shows which employers have unusual shares of pending cases on hold, at an RFI or in appeal.

## What this guide can't tell you

- **Whether your employer's layoff is inside the 656.17(k) window for your PERM.** That's dates and job titles the attorney has.
- **Whether your job will be cut.** Nothing in DOL's record predicts it.
- **Whether a new job is "same or similar" for portability.** USCIS decides that on Supplement J.

Nothing here is legal advice. Check your own dates with the attorney on the case.
