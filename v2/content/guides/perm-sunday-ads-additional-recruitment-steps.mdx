This guide is for the attorney, paralegal or HR lead planning a PERM recruitment for a professional occupation. A professional case needs two mandatory steps, a job order and two print ads, plus three more chosen from a list of ten, each with its own documentation and a timing rule that differs between the two groups.

<Callout type="important" title="The one rule most schedules get wrong">
For the three additional steps: "Only one of the additional steps may consist solely of activity that took place within 30 days of the filing of the application. None of the steps may have taken place more than 180 days prior to filing the application." (20 C.F.R. § 656.17(e)(1)(ii) (2025))
</Callout>

## The two mandatory steps

A professional occupation is one "for which the attainment of a bachelor's or higher degree is a usual education requirement" (20 C.F.R. § 656.3). For it, § 656.17(e)(1)(i) makes a job order and two print ads mandatory, conducted "at least 30 days, but no more than 180 days, before the filing of the application."

Section H.c of the ETA-9089 asks for the job order's start and end dates, whether the area has a Sunday paper, and each ad's publication and date. Those entries are the record DOL reads first.

## What every ad has to say

Under § 656.17(f), an ad in a newspaper or professional journal must:

1. Name the employer.
2. Tell applicants to report or send resumes to the employer.
3. Describe the vacancy specifically enough to tell U.S. workers what the job is.
4. Give the geographic area with enough detail to show any travel and where the worker will likely have to live.
5. Not list a wage below the prevailing wage.
6. Not list requirements or duties beyond those on the ETA-9089.
7. Not offer wages or terms less favorable than those offered to the foreign worker.

Check item 6 line by line against the form before the ad runs, because a published ad can't be edited afterward.

## The ten additional steps, and how each is proved

## The timing, worked on dates

<ComparisonTable
  headers={["Group", "Earliest", "Latest", "Rule"]}
  rows={[

The 180 days count back from filing to the first of these steps: the job order, a Sunday ad or an additional step. The notice of filing isn't one of them. Its own rule, in § 656.10(d)(3)(iv), is that it must "Be provided between 30 and 180 days before filing the application."

Say the employer plans to file on Friday, May 15, 2026. The 30-day line falls on Wednesday, April 15, 2026, and the 180-day line on Sunday, November 16, 2025.

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Job order: Monday, March 2 to Wednesday, April 1, 2026">
Inside both limits. Counting 30 days from the job order's end date, the earliest filing date is Friday, May 1, 2026, so May 15 works.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Sunday ads: March 8 and March 15, 2026">
Two different Sundays, both well over 30 days before filing.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Additional step 1: employer's website, March 2 to May 15">
It runs into the last 30 days, but it doesn't consist solely of activity inside them, so it doesn't use up the one exception.
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Additional step 2: job search website, March 9 to April 8">
Over before the 30-day line.
</Step>
<Step number={5} title="Additional step 3: referral program memo dated Monday, April 20">
Entirely inside the last 30 days. That's the one step the rule allows there. A job fair on Friday, May 1 would be a second, and it couldn't count as one of the three.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

This example counts 30 days from the day the last mandatory step ended. The regulation says the steps must be "conducted" at least 30 days before filing, and the attorney on the case decides how to measure it.

## A nonprofessional job is simpler

For a nonprofessional occupation, § 656.17(e)(2) requires at a minimum the job order and two Sunday newspaper ads, on the same 30 and 180 day rule. The ten additional steps don't apply.

## What isn't settled yet

DOL's Fall 2025 regulatory agenda lists a planned PERM proposal (RIN 1205-AC29) to change "the minimum standards for recruiting qualified U.S. workers." The Federal Register held no document under that RIN as of October 16, 2026, so none of it has been proposed. The [policy changes page](/policy-changes) lists Federal Register documents as they appear.

## What this guide can't decide

- Which newspaper is "most appropriate to the occupation" for your job. That's a judgment about the area and the field, made by the attorney on the case. Nothing here is legal advice.
- Whether a web posting that ran for one day counts the same as one that ran for a month. The rule asks for dated pages; how long they ran is the case's own question.
