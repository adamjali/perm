There are around twenty data pages here. Most people need two of them, and which two depends on whether you're holding a case number or trying to understand the queue around it.

This is the whole map, one line per page. No account is needed for any of it.

## If you have a case number

Everything starts at the [case status lookup](/perm-case-status). One box takes every DOL case number: PERM (`G-100-`, and the older `A-` form), prevailing wage requests (`P-100-`), and H-1B labor condition applications (`I-200-` and `I-203-`). It works out the program from the prefix.

Pending cases are looked up for all three. If the number isn't already held here, the site asks DOL's own case system at that moment and records the answer, so a case filed last week still resolves.

| Page | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| [Case status](/perm-case-status) | What does DOL say about this exact case number today? |
| [Case search](/perm-cases) | Which PERM cases match this employer, firm, state or occupation? |
| [Wage requests](/pwd-cases) | Which ETA-9141 requests has this employer filed, and what wage came back? |
| [H-1B LCAs](/lca-cases) | Which labor condition applications has this employer filed? |
| [Calculators](/calculators) | Given my dates, what are my deadlines and my likely decision window? |

On a case that can still change, you can leave an email address and hear when DOL's status moves. It works for all three programs, it's double opt-in, and it's one email per change.

Two things worth knowing before you read a queue figure. DOL clears a filing month over weeks, so being in the current month doesn't mean this week. And DOL runs two prevailing wage queues, one for cases using the standard federal wage survey and one for everything else. They move at different speeds and a case record doesn't say which queue a request is in, so both get shown.

One caution that applies to all four. DOL prints some large firms under several spellings, so a single practice can appear more than once with its volume split between the entries. Treat a rank as approximate.

## If you want to know what gets denied

| Page | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| [Denial rates](/perm-denial-risk) | Which measured factors go with a higher denial rate? |
| [RFIs and audits](/perm-rfi-audit) | How many cases sit at each review stage, and what happens after an RFI? |

We publish the measured rates and refuse to blend them into a single risk score. The factors aren't independent, so one number would read as precision the data can't support.

## What's actually behind all of it

| Holding | Size |
|---|---|
| Decided prevailing wage determinations | about 634,600, with the wage, occupation, worksite state and validity |
| PERM case statuses | about 414,000 |
| Live wage requests | about 20,000 |
| H-1B LCAs | about 49,000 |
| Visa bulletin history | every bulletin since October 2014 |

Decided detail runs through 30 June 2026, and received dates go back to 2021. Everything refreshes daily from DOL.

## What none of these pages can tell you

- **A date for your case.** The estimates are estimates. Every date is labelled as one, and graded against real decisions on the [estimate scorecard](/estimate-scorecard).
- **Which wage queue a request is in.** DOL doesn't put it on the record.
- **Why.** DOL publishes what it decided, never its reasoning. No page here can tell you why a case was audited or denied.
- **Anything current in the decided files.** Wage, occupation and duration arrive with DOL's quarterly publication, so a case decided last month has a live status here and no published detail yet.
- **What your employer or attorney intends.** A filing is a legal step, not a plan you can read.
