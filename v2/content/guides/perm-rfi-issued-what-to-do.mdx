An RFI is a question. The verdict comes later. The analyst reviewing the application wants something clarified before deciding it, and the case leaves the ordinary queue until the answer arrives. That's the whole of what the status says. Everything else people read into it, including that the case is in trouble, isn't in the record.

## What DOL is doing

The status itself comes from DOL. Its own case search, on flag.dol.gov, answers for a case number, and RFI ISSUED is what it prints.

The regulation, 20 CFR 656.20(d), lets the Certifying Officer request supplemental information or documentation at any point before a final determination. That's the hook. The letters themselves ask about the job: why a requirement is normal for the occupation, what a duty involves, whether a worksite is what the form says. Sometimes the question is about the recruitment, but that's more often an audit's territory.

An RFI is lighter than an audit, and DOL runs the two as separate statuses in separate queues. An audit calls in the whole recruitment file under 656.20(a) and carries a 30-day clock, which the Certifying Officer may extend once, by up to 30 days. An RFI asks a question and the letter sets its own date. If your status is PENDING AUDIT RESPONSE rather than RFI ISSUED, you're in the other process, and the [RFI deadline calculator](/tools/rfi-deadline) counts the audit's 30 days.

## Where the deadline comes from

The regulation sets no window for an RFI. The letter sets the date. In practice DOL gives 30 days: OFLC said so at a stakeholder session on September 27, 2024 (AILA's notes of it, document 24101705), and our own record agrees. That has two consequences:

- The date on the letter is the date that counts. The day anyone opened it doesn't change it.
- Nothing on a public page can tell you the deadline for your case for certain. Only the letter and the attorney who received it can.

A missed deadline is how a case that was going to be certified gets denied instead. The response goes back through whoever filed the case; a beneficiary can't answer an RFI directly, because the employer is the applicant.

## What you can do while it's open

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Get the letter's date and its question from the attorney">
The letter went to the employer. Ask for the date on the letter and what it asks. The date is your deadline. The question tells you whether anything is needed from you. Usually nothing is. Sometimes it's a résumé detail or a degree evaluation.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Watch the status for the change">
DOL doesn't announce when the answer was received or when the analyst picked it back up. The status changes when the case leaves RFI and again when the determination is made. The [case status page](/perm-case-status) checks DOL's record through the day and can email you when it moves.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Don't read the wait as a signal">
Most cases leave RFI status when the 30 days run out and are decided within days of going back to review. Some take longer. A long wait after the response goes in is the analyst's queue. It says nothing about the answer.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What this guide can't tell you

- Why your case got an RFI. DOL doesn't publish the reason, and the letter is the only record of it.
- Your deadline. It's on the letter and nowhere else.
- Whether your case will be certified. The share certified is a population rate, and the population is the cases already decided.
