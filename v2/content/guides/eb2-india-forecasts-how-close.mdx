If you were born in India and hold an EB-2 priority date, you've seen predictions for the month it'll be current. This guide doesn't add another one. It shows where the line stands in the State Department's own bulletin, how much it has jumped around, and how far one common way of estimating the wait has missed when it was tested against bulletins that already happened.

<Callout type="stat" title="EB-2 India, October 2026 bulletin" stat="Nov 1, 2013">
The final action date, which decides when a green card can be approved. The dates for filing chart, which decides when an I-485 can be filed when USCIS allows it, stands at January 15, 2015. Both read from the [EB-2 India line](/visa-bulletin/categories) on October 16, 2026.
</Callout>

## How the line has moved, a fiscal year at a time

The State Department plans visa numbers by fiscal year, which starts in October. Comparing each year's first bulletin with its last shows how uneven the movement is.

Since October 2014 the EB-2 India cutoff moved 54 months of priority dates across 144 months of bulletins, and it went backwards or shut 10 times, most recently in July 2026. A line that gains three years in one fiscal year and loses more than a year in the next is hard to extrapolate, whoever's doing it.

November alone shows the spread. Of the 17 earlier November bulletins in the archive, 8 moved EB-2 India forward, 8 held it, and 1 sent it back. The median move was zero days, and the range ran from 1,536 days back to 457 days forward.

## Why every forecast is a guess

The cutoff depends on numbers nobody knows in advance:

- **The yearly supply.** For fiscal year 2026 the State Department's [Annual Numerical Limits sheet](https://adoption.state.gov/content/dam/visas/Statistics/Immigrant-Statistics/Annual%20%20Numerical%20%20Limits%20-%20FY2026.pdf) set the employment-based total at 186,000 worldwide, with 53,196 for EB-2. The total is above the statutory 140,000 because unused family numbers carried over, and the sheet marks the figures "Estimated, pending official determination."
- **The per-country limit.** The same sheet allows any one country 3,724 EB-2 numbers, and 13,020 across all employment categories.
- **Who files and when.** USCIS counts I-485s already filed, and approved petitions still waiting, but people move between categories, change employers, give up, or hold two petitions.
- **How the State Department spends the numbers month to month.** It sets the cutoffs and publishes no forecast of its own.

A forecast picks values for all four. When one of them turns out differently, the date moves.

## How far a pace estimate has missed

One common method takes the gap between the cutoff and your priority date and divides it by the pace the cutoff has moved over past years. Two of this site's tools print a figure of that kind, so the same arithmetic is replayed on every bulletin since October 2016 and checked against what the State Department actually did next.

Those figures come from 1,829 past bulletin readings across every employment category and country with a dated cutoff, as the test stood on October 16, 2026. They aren't EB-2 India alone, and EB-2 India's jumps are larger than most lines'.

Two things follow. The further your date sits past the cutoff, the less any pace-based date means: two years out, the typical miss is over a year. And an EB-2 India priority date from 2016 or later sits more than two years past this month's final action date, beyond the longest distance tested.

<Callout type="warning" title="A forecast with no stated record">
If a site gives you a month without saying how its past forecasts did, you can't tell a careful estimate from a guess. The test above is how this site checks its own arithmetic, and it's the reason the tools here print the miss beside the figure.
</Callout>

## Who's ahead of you

The bulletin decides the timing. USCIS's counts say how many people stand in the line:

| What USCIS counts for EB-2 India | Figure | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Approved petitions waiting for a visa number (primary applicants) | 356,360 | June 2026 |
| I-485s pending and waiting for a visa number | 27,785 to 28,226 | August 5, 2026 |
| I-485s pending with an earlier priority date than the October final action date | 3,519 to 3,960 | August 5, 2026 |

The I-485 counts are ranges because USCIS withholds any cell holding 1 to 10 applications. The [I-485 queue position tool](/tools/i485-queue-position) gives the count ahead of your own date, and [the green card line](/tools/green-card-line) adds the approved petitions that can't file yet, with spouses and children.

## What this can't tell you

- The month your date will be current. Nobody can measure that before it happens.
- Whether an EB-3 petition would be faster for you. That depends on your job's requirements; [EB-2 vs EB-3 on a PERM](/guides/eb2-vs-eb3-perm) covers the rules and both India lines side by side.
- Anything about your own case. Your attorney has the facts that matter, and nothing here is legal advice.
