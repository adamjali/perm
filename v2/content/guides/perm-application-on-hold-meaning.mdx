Your case number came back saying **APPLICATION ON HOLD** and nothing else. There's no letter, no explanation, and no official glossary that defines it.

Here's what can actually be said about it, and what can't.

## The short answer

`APPLICATION ON HOLD` means the Department of Labor has stopped work on your PERM for a reason outside the ordinary review queue. It is **not a denial**, and it is not the same thing as an audit.

Beyond that, DOL does not publish a definition. Anyone telling you precisely why your specific case is on hold is guessing, unless they've been told directly.

## How it differs from an audit and from an RFI

These three get confused constantly, and they are different things.

| Status | What it is | Does it need a response from the employer? |
|---|---|---|
| **Application on hold** | DOL has paused work on the case | Usually not, and often nothing has been asked |
| **RFI issued** | DOL has asked a specific question | Yes, with a deadline |
| **Audit** | DOL wants proof of the recruitment | Yes, and it is substantial |

The practical difference is that an audit and an RFI both put a clock on your employer. A hold usually does not, which is exactly what makes it frustrating: there is often nothing to do.

## What tends to put a case on hold

DOL doesn't publish a list of reasons. One employer-wide hold has been explained in public by DOL's own Inspector General, and the rest are the situations attorneys report:

- **An employer's filings are suspended.** On September 8, 2026, DOL's Inspector General posted on X that "@Cognizant's PERM filings are suspended" and that his office "is on the hunt" ([the post](https://x.com/USLaborIG/status/2097321316995232030)). That's the only employer-wide hold with a public explanation. An investigation isn't a finding, and DOL hasn't said anything about any other employer's holds.
- A related case for the same worker or employer is being decided first.
- A question about the employer's identity, ownership, or business status.
- A pending appeal or reconsideration elsewhere in the file.
- An administrative issue that has nothing to do with the merits.

**None of these is confirmed by DOL for your case.** If you see the status and want the actual reason, the employer's attorney can ask; you cannot.

## How long does a hold last?

Unknown, and anyone quoting a number is inventing it. A hold is not a queue with a published position, unlike ordinary analyst review, where DOL publishes the filing month it has reached.

What you *can* do is see the date DOL last changed the status, and get told the moment it changes again.

## What to do

1. **Check the status yourself.** Put your `G-` or `A-` number into [case status](/perm-case-status) for DOL's own record, with the date it was last verified.
2. **Set an alert.** Leave an email address on that page and you'll hear when DOL moves it. One message per change, no account.
3. **Ask your employer's attorney one specific question.** Not "how long will this take", which nobody knows, but "has DOL contacted us about this case, and is there anything outstanding from our side". That has an answer.
4. **Don't refile or panic.** A hold is not a denial and withdrawing to refile restarts everything, including the priority date.

## What this page can't tell you

- **Why your case specifically is on hold.** The status carries no reason, and DOL has explained only one employer's holds in public.
- **When it will move.** There is no published queue position for this status.
- **Whether it predicts the outcome.** It doesn't, in either direction.
