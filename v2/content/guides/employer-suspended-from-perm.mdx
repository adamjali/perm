This guide is for people whose green card runs through an employer whose PERM cases DOL has put on hold as a group, as it did for several large employers on October 8, 2026. News reports and law firm alerts describe a suspension and name eight employers: Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL and Capgemini. DOL hasn't confirmed that list. What a suspension means for you depends on one thing, how far your case had gone, so the answer below is by stage.

<Callout type="warning" title="What DOL has said in writing, as of October 16, 2026">
Nothing published. There's no notice in the Federal Register and nothing on OFLC's announcements pages, so there's no stated reason, scope or end date. What does exist is DOL's own case system, which shows the holds case by case. The counts on this site come from there.
</Callout>

## The cases on hold today

DOL's own case system shows the holds case by case, and the counts below come from it.

DOL's moves are dated in this site's daily record. On October 8, 2026 it put 598 of Microsoft's pending cases on hold, 169 of Capgemini's, 10 of Adobe's and 7 of Tata Consultancy Services'. Adobe's larger group went on hold on September 24, and Cognizant's cases were already held when the record began in late August. The [employers under review page](/perm-employers/under-review) shows each move by day, and you can follow an employer there to get an email when DOL moves its cases as a group.

## The rule this most likely runs under

DOL hasn't named it, and law firms point to [20 CFR 656.31(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-D/section-656.31). It lets DOL "suspend processing of any permanent labor certification application involving such employer ... until completion of any investigation and/or judicial proceedings". It also sets a first checkpoint: a suspension "may last initially for up to 180 days", after which the National Certifying Officer "may resume processing some or all of the applications, or may extend the suspension".

If the suspension began on October 8, the 180th day is Tuesday, April 6, 2027. That's the first point at which the regulation asks for a decision. It isn't a promised end date.

DOL has used this kind of suspension before. Its news release of May 12, 2026 (release 26-734-NAT) announced it was suspending "processing of all permanent labor certification applications filed by, or on behalf of, Cloudera" for 180 days, with a possible extension pending a Justice Department investigation.

## What it means at your stage

It depends on how far your case had gone. A PERM on hold keeps its filing date, a certified one's 180-day I-140 window keeps running, and the later stages are USCIS's.

Two points need a closer look.

**The I-140 rows carry one real risk.** A priority date is kept after a withdrawal, but not if USCIS revokes the approval because of fraud, a DOL revocation or invalidation of the labor certification, or a material error (8 CFR 204.5(e)(2)). An investigation isn't a finding, and nothing like that has been announced for any case. It's the reason the investigation matters to people whose PERM was certified long ago.

**The H-1B extensions keep working.** The one-year extensions past six years depend on a PERM or I-140 filed at least 365 days earlier, and they run "until ... a final decision has been made" to deny it (8 CFR 214.2(h)(13)(iii)(D)(2)). A hold isn't a final decision. The new H-1B employer doesn't have to be the one that filed the PERM ((D)(6)). The [six-year guide](/guides/h1b-six-year-limit-with-perm-pending) works through the dates.

## What people are saying, against the regulation

Three things people are saying don't hold up against the regulation: a hold doesn't end the H-1B extensions, a layoff doesn't bar a filing, and a filed PERM keeps its filing date.

| What's said | What the regulation says |
|---|---|
| Workers at these companies can't stay past six years. | The 365-day rule turns on a PERM or I-140 filed long enough ago and not finally denied. A hold doesn't end it. |
| A company with mass layoffs can't file PERM for 180 days. | 20 CFR 656.17(k) makes an employer notify and consider workers it laid off in the occupation and area within six months. It adds a step. It doesn't bar the filing. |
| My priority date is gone. | A filed PERM keeps its filing date while it waits. An approved I-140's date is kept for a later petition except for the four reasons in 204.5(e)(2). |

## What to do now

Check your own case, write down your dates, get told when something moves, and ask the attorney three questions.

<StepByStep>
  <Step number={1} title="Check your own case">
    Put your case number into [case status](/perm-case-status) to see DOL's current status and the date it last changed. If you don't have the number, [find it by employer](/case-search).
  </Step>
  <Step number={2} title="Write down your dates">
    The PERM filing date, the I-140 approval date if you have one, the I-485 receipt date, and the date your H-1B time runs out. Every rule above turns on one of them.
  </Step>
  <Step number={3} title="Get told when something moves">
    Leave an email on your case page for that case, and follow the employer on its page for group moves. One email per change.
  </Step>
  <Step number={4} title="Ask the attorney three questions">
    Has the employer received a written notice of suspension? Is my case one of those held? If my I-140 is approved, when does it pass 180 days, and has my I-485 been pending 180 days?
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

Nothing here is legal advice. Your options depend on your dates, and the attorney has them.

## What this guide can't tell you

This guide can't tell you why DOL suspended these employers, when the suspension ends, or whether held cases will be decided on their merits afterward.

- **Why DOL suspended these employers.** DOL attaches no reason to a status, and the eight employers' cases have no published explanation. DOL's Inspector General posted about Cognizant on September 8, 2026, and the [on-hold guide](/guides/perm-application-on-hold-meaning) quotes it.
- **When it ends.** There's no stated end date. The 180-day point is a checkpoint.
- **Whether held cases will be decided on their merits afterward.** The regulation provides for that when charges end without a finding of fraud (656.31(d)). Nothing has been said about these cases.
