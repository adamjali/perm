This guide is for anyone renewing a work permit while a green card case waits: people with a pending I-485, and H-4 spouses whose work permit rests on the H-1B's approved I-140. The change is simple to state. A renewal filed now no longer keeps your old card working while USCIS decides it.

<Callout type="important" title="The rule, and its two dates">
**October 30, 2025:** an interim final rule (the IFR) ended automatic extensions for renewals filed on or after that day (90 FR 48799). **October 14, 2026:** DHS published the final rule, Federal Register document 2026-20955 (91 FR 65306), effective that day. It "adopts the IFR as final without change." So the date that decides your case is the day your renewal was filed.
</Callout>

## Which rule covers your renewal

For categories where USCIS grants the permission to work separately, which includes a pending I-485 (category C09) and an H-4 spouse (C26), 274a.13(e)(2) says the card "will expire, and the attendant employment authorization will terminate, the day after the end validity date". The text makes exceptions only for what another law provides and for Temporary Protected Status, which follows its own Federal Register notices.

The [EAD extension checker](/tools/ead-extension) tells you which side of the line an older renewal falls on, and the date its extension runs to.

## Who it hits hardest

- **Anyone working on the EAD alone.** A pending I-485 applicant who has let H-1B status go, or an H-4 spouse, stops working the day after the card ends if the new card hasn't come.
- **Less so, H-1B holders with a pending I-485.** If you still hold H-1B status, you can keep working for the H-1B employer on that status (8 CFR 274a.12(b)(9)) while the EAD renewal waits. Keeping the H-1B current is the insurance.
- **The final rule's own example.** A pending I-485 applicant's EAD expires May 30, 2026, and the renewal went in on February 4, 2026. If USCIS hasn't decided it by May 31, that person can't keep working from May 31 on, "unless the alien is employment authorized on a separate basis." Approval later lets them work again, from the new card's dates.

## When to file

USCIS's Form I-765 page (last updated October 7, 2026) says: "We generally recommend you file a Form I-765 to request a renewal Employment Authorization Document (EAD) up to 180 days before your current EAD expires." It adds that USCIS generally doesn't backdate or postdate the new card to the end of the old one, so the new card's dates start when it's approved.

<StepByStep>
  <Step number={1} title="Find your card's end date">
    It's printed on the card as "Card Expires". Say it's Wednesday, March 31, 2027.
  </Step>
  <Step number={2} title="Count back 180 days">
    For that card, 180 days earlier is Friday, October 2, 2026. That's the earliest date USCIS recommends filing.
  </Step>
  <Step number={3} title="File as close to that date as you can">
    Every week you wait is a week less margin. The final rule says applicants can lower the risk by filing "as early as permitted under applicable regulations and form instructions" and by including all the evidence up front, so the case isn't slowed by a request for evidence.
  </Step>
  <Step number={4} title="Compare the margin with USCIS's current wait">
    The [USCIS processing times page](/uscis-processing-times) shows how long USCIS has been taking on the I-765 from its own quarterly figures. If the wait is longer than your margin, plan for a gap with your employer now, before the card ends.
  </Step>
</StepByStep>

## H-4 spouses

An H-4 spouse can get a work permit only if the H-1B is the beneficiary of an approved I-140, or has H-1B time extended past six years under AC21 (8 CFR 214.2(h)(9)(iv)). USCIS's I-765 page says the H-4 application can be filed "at the same time as your Form I-539 and your H1-B spouse's Form I-129". If the H-4 status itself needs extending, file both as early as the rules allow.

## What doesn't change

- **Your pending I-485.** Nothing about it moves. The work permit is a separate application.
- **The 180-day recommendation.** It stands. What's gone is the extension that used to cover the wait.
- **Advance parole.** A separate form, the I-131, with its own processing time. The [EAD and advance parole guide](/guides/ead-and-advance-parole-timelines) covers both.

Nothing here is legal advice. If you're close to a gap, the employer's attorney can say whether anything else covers your work.

## What this guide can't tell you

- **How long your renewal will take.** USCIS's figures describe its recent pace across many cases. Yours can take more or less time.
- **Whether the rule will change again.** The [policy changes page](/policy-changes) carries the Federal Register's documents as they're published.
- **Your own category's details.** USCIS's form instructions set the rules for each category, and some categories differ.
