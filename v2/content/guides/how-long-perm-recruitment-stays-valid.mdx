This guide is for the attorney, paralegal or HR lead holding a finished PERM recruitment and deciding when it has to be filed. The short answer: the ETA-9089 can be filed at least 30 days after the last mandatory recruitment step and within 180 days of the first (20 C.F.R. § 656.17(e) (2025)). Outside those two lines, the recruitment can't support a filing. The first step is the first of the § 656.17(e) steps: the job order, a Sunday ad or an additional step. The notice of filing is never the first step; it has its own rule in § 656.10(d).

<Callout type="stat" stat="30 to 180" title="Days before filing, for every mandatory step">
"The mandatory recruitment steps must be conducted at least 30 days, but no more than 180 days, before the filing of the application." For the additional steps: "None of the steps may have taken place more than 180 days prior to filing the application." (20 C.F.R. § 656.17(e)(1)(i) and (ii))
</Callout>

So the first § 656.17(e) step starts a 180-day clock, and the last mandatory step starts a 30-day wait. Filing has to land after the wait and before the clock runs out.

<Step number={3} title="Earliest filing: Friday, May 1, 2026">
30 days after April 1.
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Latest filing: Saturday, August 29, 2026">
180 days after March 2. A Saturday still works: "Electronically filed applications will be considered filed when submitted" (§ 656.17(c)).
</Step>

That's about four months of room. The three additional steps have to fit inside it too, and the notice of filing has to meet its own rule in § 656.10(d).

## Example 2: a late ad leaves no day to file

Keep everything from Example 1, but move the second Sunday ad to Sunday, August 2, 2026. The earliest filing date becomes Tuesday, September 1, 2026, which is after the last day the March 2 job order allows. No filing date satisfies both rules, so the recruitment can't be used.

The arithmetic behind it: the last mandatory step has to end by 150 days after the first, or the 30-day wait pushes filing past day 180. In Example 1 that date is Thursday, July 30, 2026.

## Example 3: a filing that slips

Keep Example 1's recruitment and file on Tuesday, September 1, 2026. The job order began 183 days earlier, past the 180-day limit, and the recruitment is stale. A new recruitment, with a new job order and new ads, is the way forward.

## How the days are counted here

These examples count 30 days from the day the last mandatory step ended (for the job order, its end date) and 180 days from the day the first § 656.17(e) step began. The regulation says the steps must be "conducted" within those limits, and the attorney on the case decides how to measure them. Nothing here is legal advice.

## What this guide can't tell you

- Whether a change to a job is big enough to need new recruitment. That's a judgment on the duties and requirements, made by the attorney.
- When DOL will decide the case once it's filed. Filing ends the recruitment's clock; DOL's queue starts its own. <DolNow />
