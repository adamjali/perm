This guide is for the attorney, paralegal or HR lead holding a PERM audit letter. The deadline runs 30 days from the date printed on the letter, the Certifying Officer can grant one extension of up to 30 days, and an answer that misses the date costs the case and the appeal with it.

<Callout type="important" title="What the audit letter does, 20 C.F.R. § 656.20(a)">
It will "State the documentation that must be submitted by the employer" and "Specify a date, 30 days from the date of the audit letter, by which the required documentation must be submitted." It also warns that the application will be denied if the documentation isn't sent by that date.
</Callout>

## The dates, worked

The response is due 30 days from the date printed on the audit letter, and the officer may grant one extension of up to 30 more days.

The [RFI and audit deadline calculator](/tools/rfi-deadline) counts the 30 days from the letter's date and gives the weekday. The regulation allows "one extension, of up to 30 days," at the officer's discretion and names no procedure for asking, so ask well inside the first 30 days and keep working on the response as if it won't be granted.

## Working the response

Answer every item on the letter's list, diary the due date from the letter's own date, and build the response from the file the employer already kept.

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Read the letter's list first">
The letter states the documentation it wants (§ 656.20(a)(1)). That list is the scope of the response. Answer every item on it, in its order.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Diary the date from the letter">
Put the due date on the case the day the letter arrives, counted from the letter's own date. Add a reminder a week before.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Pull the audit file">
Everything an audit can ask for should already be in the file the employer kept under § 656.10(f). The [document checklist](/guides/perm-document-checklist) lists what each stage keeps.
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Check the documents against the application">
The ads' requirements against the ETA-9089 (§ 656.17(f)(6)), the job order dates against Section H, the recruitment report's counts against the resumes. A mismatch found now can be explained in the response itself.
</Step>
<Step number={5} title="Send it with an index, and keep proof it was sent">
A cover index that follows the letter's list, and a record of when and how the response went in.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What a late answer costs

Missing the date costs more than the application. Under § 656.20(a)(3), failing to provide the documentation in time "constitutes a refusal to exhaust available administrative remedies," and the review procedure in § 656.26 is then closed to the employer. So the case is denied and the route to the Board of Alien Labor Certification Appeals is closed.

There's a second cost further out. "A substantial failure by the employer to provide required documentation will result in that application being denied" and may lead the officer to require supervised recruitment on the employer's future filings "for up to 2 years" (§§ 656.20(b), 656.24(f)). That would reach the employer's other filings for the length of the order.

## After the response goes in

Before deciding, the officer may request more information or require supervised recruitment for the pending application (§ 656.20(d)). If the case is denied, a request for reconsideration may include only documents DOL already received in response to its own request, or documents that existed at filing and were kept under § 656.10(f) (§ 656.24(g)(2)). That's why the audit file is built before any letter arrives.

## Audit or RFI

An audit is the formal instrument under § 656.20(a), with the fixed 30 days. A request for information under § 656.20(d) carries whatever date its own letter gives. If the case status reads RFI ISSUED rather than an audit, the [RFI guide](/guides/perm-rfi-issued-what-to-do) covers that process.

## What the record shows

DOL publishes no audit rate. Its processing-times figures do name the filing month its Audit Review queue is working, and the [processing times page](/perm-processing-times) shows that month beside the main queue. The [RFIs, audits and appeals page](/perm-rfi-audit) counts the cases at each review stage from DOL's case-status service, and what has happened to the RFIs this site watched.

## What this guide can't do

It can't tell you what a particular letter asks for, or predict the outcome.

- Tell you what a particular letter asks for. The letter says it, and the attorney on the case answers it. Nothing here is legal advice.
- Predict the outcome. An audit is a request for proof; the decision depends on what the file shows.
