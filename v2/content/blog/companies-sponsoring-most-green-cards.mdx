The companies sponsoring the most employment-based green cards aren’t the
ones most people would guess. Microsoft leads the current window with 4,840
PERM filings, but half of the top ten are food processors, staffing firms and
trailer manufacturers, because PERM is how employers of every kind hire
permanently when the domestic pipeline can’t fill the role.

Everything below comes from the Department of Labor's own quarterly
disclosure files: 259,489 unique decided cases, unioned across quarters and
de-duplicated by case number. Nothing is modeled. The live, searchable
version of this table covers the top hundred on our
[employers page](/perm-employers).

Names appear exactly as DOL prints them, which is why they read as legal
entities. A company you know by one name can file under several.

## What the list actually says

**Tech is the volume leader, not the whole story.** Microsoft, Oracle,
JPMorgan Chase and NVIDIA all sit in the top eleven, and Software Developers
alone account for 57,876 filings, by far the largest occupation. But a beef
processor in Georgia, a catfish producer in Mississippi and a trailer plant
in Wisconsin each out-file Walmart. Sponsorship concentration follows labor
shortage, not industry glamour.

**Approval rates run high for everyone.** Nearly every top sponsor certifies
above 99%. That’s what the process looks like when recruitment is run
correctly at scale: PERM denials concentrate in one-off filings with
documentation problems, not in the high-volume programs. The visible
exception is NVIDIA at 92.8% in this window; the files record the outcome,
not the reason, so we won’t guess at one.

<Callout type="info">
  A big sponsor isn’t a faster one. DOL works a single national queue,
  oldest first, whoever filed the case. Your employer's volume says how
  practiced their process is; your own decision date depends on your filing
  month, which the <a href="/tools/perm-timeline-calculator">decision
  estimator</a> reads against the live queue.
</Callout>

## How to use this if you’re the beneficiary

Search your employer on the [full top-100 table](/perm-employers). Three
things are worth reading off their row: filings (a practiced program), the
approval rate (a clean one), and the median days (which will sit near the
national median, because the queue is national). If your employer isn’t in
the top hundred, that says nothing bad. The field has tens of thousands of
sponsors, and most file a handful of cases a year.

## How to use this if you’re filing

The medians are the benchmark: the current national median runs around 480
days from filing to decision, and it barely moves by state or by sponsor.
The [state map](/perm-by-state) and the [wages page](/perm-wages) carry the
other two cuts of the same files, and the [methodology](/methodology) shows
every figure's recipe.
