Two questions decide whether a PERM case ends in EB-2 or EB-3: what the job requires, and where each line stands for the worker's country. This note answers both with figures read live from the records, so it's current on the day you read it.

A PERM doesn't carry a category. The I-140 filed on it does, and the job's minimum requirements, as written on the ETA-9089, decide which categories it can use. The [EB-2 or EB-3 guide](/guides/eb2-vs-eb3-perm) covers the rules and the downgrade; this note covers the numbers.

## How many PERM jobs meet the EB-2 bar?

The figure below counts recent PERM jobs whose own requirements meet the EB-2 bar: a master's or higher, or a bachelor's and five years of experience. It reads each job's requirements from its wage request, in DOL's published files.

A job that meets the bar can still be filed in EB-3, since the employer chooses the category when it files the I-140. So the split is what these jobs could file, never what they did.

## Which line did the I-140s actually go to?

It depends more on the worker's country than on the job. USCIS counts every I-140 it approves by class and by the beneficiary's country of birth, and the split below is what employers filed on a PERM in the newest complete fiscal year.

The national interest waiver is left out, because it needs no PERM. Each bar counts petitions USCIS received that year and has approved since, so a year's figures still rise a little as its last petitions are decided.

## Why does the answer change by country?

Both categories draw on the same share of the yearly employment-based total, and a cap limits each country's use of it. So a country with heavy demand can wait years in both lines while others are current.

For India and China, which line is ahead changes, and each change has brought a wave of downgrade petitions. India's own years show the biggest one.

<EbApprovalsNow country="India" />

From the August 2020 bulletin to November 2021, EB-3's final action date for India ran ahead of EB-2's. By September 2021 it stood at January 1, 2014, against September 1, 2011 for EB-2. Many employers filed a second, EB-3 petition for workers already approved in EB-2, and the fiscal 2021 column is the result. EB-2 moved back ahead in the December 2021 bulletin, and the rush eased. Switch the country in the comparison above to see where each line stands today.

## What this can't tell you

This note can't tell you whether your own job's requirements support EB-2, which line will move first next year, or whether a downgrade is worth its fee for your case. Those are questions for the attorney on the case. Nothing here is legal advice.
