If you work for an H-1B or green card sponsor, a layoff notice is one of the few public signs of a job cut before it happens. This note lists the notices filed so far in October 2026 by employers that also appear in DOL's H-1B or PERM records, and what those records show. It reports the filings as the states printed them and says nothing about why any employer made its decision.

<Callout type="info" title="Where these notices come from">
The WARN Act makes an employer give "notification 60 calendar days in advance of plant closings and mass layoffs" ([20 CFR 639.1(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-639/section-639.1)), and some states publish the notices. When this note was written, the site read four: California, New York, Texas and Washington, held to October 14, 2026. It reads 47 states and DC now, and [layoff notices](/layoffs) lists them. Each is matched to DOL's records by the employer's name. Read from that page on October 16, 2026.
</Callout>

## What the rules say a layoff touches

A layoff touches three things: a PERM the employer files later, its H-1B filings, and a worker who loses the job.

- **A PERM filed later.** If the employer had a layoff in the area of intended employment within 6 months of filing, in the occupation or a related one, it "must document it has notified and considered all potentially qualified laid off" U.S. workers ([20 CFR 656.17(k)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-C/section-656.17)). The rule adds a recruitment step. It doesn't bar the filing.
- **H-1B filings.** [Executive Order 14431](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/23/2026-19555/enhancing-program-integrity-and-interagency-coordination-in-the-administration-of-the-h-1b), published September 23, 2026, tells State, Labor and Homeland Security to take into account on any LCA, H-1B petition or visa whether the employer laid off similarly situated US workers in the previous year or plans to. No rule saying how that's weighed had been published by October 16.
- **A worker who loses the job.** The H-1B grace period of up to 60 days still applies; DHS has proposed removing it, with comments open to November 10, 2026. [Employer layoffs and your PERM](/guides/employer-layoffs-and-your-perm) walks through each stage of a green card case.

## What these notices can't tell you

A notice can't say which jobs were cut, what was filed outside the four states, or whether a matched name is always the same company.

- Which jobs were cut. A notice gives a count and a site, never the roles or the visas of the people in it.
- Anything outside those four states. A sponsor with no notice here may have filed one in another state, or none was required.
- Whether a matched name is the same company in every case. Matching is by name, and each employer's page names the notices it carries.
