## What stands out this quarter?

DOL decided about a quarter more PERM cases than in January to March, and the cases it certified had waited about two months less. Five things stand out, all from the file:

- **More decisions, and a faster middle.** The median certified case took about two months less from filing than in the quarter before or a year before.
- **More denials.** DOL certified a smaller share of what it decided: denials were more than three times what they were a year before.
- **Big employers back in volume.** Microsoft's decided cases more than doubled on the quarter before. Tesla went from one decided case in January to March to hundreds, nearly all in May and June. Deloitte Consulting had one decided case a year before and hundreds this quarter.
- **The H-1B season.** April to June is when the lottery's winners file, and DOL certified about twice as many H-1B LCAs as in January to March, about as many as a year before.
- **Fewer wage requests.** DOL determined fewer prevailing wage requests than in either the quarter before or a year before.

## What this file can't tell you

The file holds decided cases only, so it says nothing about the cases still waiting. The [PERM queue](/perm-queue) counts those from DOL's live record, by the month they were filed.

It counts each case on the day DOL decided it. A busy quarter here may be DOL working through an older filing month. The wait figures say how old the decided cases were.

DOL doesn't publish why it denied a case, and a withdrawal is the employer's own choice. An employer that files under several names is counted on one page, the way its page counts it; a name DOL's file spells in a way this site can't tie to a page stays apart.

## Where the figures come from

Every figure is from DOL's quarterly PERM, prevailing wage and LCA disclosure files, counted by the day DOL decided each case. The [PERM case search](/case-search) reads the same files one case at a time, and each employer and law firm links to its full record.
