If you file PERM cases, or you're waiting on one, you've probably heard that DOL is rewriting the PERM rules. Here's what DOL itself has said about the proposal, where it stands in federal review, and what it changes for a case today, which is nothing.

<Callout type="stat" stat="Cleared review" title="Where it stands on October 27, 2026">
The White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) finished its review of the draft proposed rule on October 23, 2026. It had received the draft on September 14. The Federal Register still holds no document under RIN 1205-AC29, so there's no proposed text to read and no comment period open yet.
</Callout>

## Where it stands

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Listed in the Unified Agenda: done">
DOL's entry on reginfo.gov names the rule "Modernizing the Labor Market Test and Improving Protections for U.S. Workers in the PERM Immigrant Visa Program", at the proposed rule stage, under 20 CFR 656. Its timetable listed the proposal for July 2026.
</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Review at OIRA: done">
OIRA's own record shows RIN 1205-AC29 received on September 14, 2026, stage "Proposed Rule", and its review completed on October 23, 2026, with the decision recorded as "Consistent with Change". What changed in the draft won't be public until the proposal is published.
</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Published as a proposed rule: next">
Not yet. On October 27, 2026, neither the Federal Register nor its list of documents on public inspection held anything under this number. When it's published, the notice will carry the proposed text and the comment deadline.
</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Comments, then a final rule: later">
DOL has to consider the comments before it can publish a final rule, and only a final rule changes 20 CFR 656. The final rule sets its own effective date.
</Step>
</StepByStep>

## What DOL says it covers

The agenda entry is a summary, and it's the only official description so far. It says DOL "will seek to modernize the standards and procedures by which the Department receives and reviews employers' applications for permanent labor certification", and names four areas. Here's each one beside the rule that governs it today.

The entry also gives DOL's reason: the PERM regulations haven't been comprehensively revised since 2004, and technology has changed how employers recruit since then. It lists the legal authority as 8 U.S.C. 1182(a)(5)(A) and 1182(p)(1), and DOL's priority as "Other Significant".

## What nobody knows yet

The agenda entry doesn't say which recruitment steps would change, whether print advertising stays, how a layoff would be defined, how long records would be kept, or whether a final rule would reach cases already filed. Until the proposed text is published, any detail past those four areas is a guess, and we won't add one.

## A different proposal that's easy to confuse with it

DOL published a separate PERM proposal on March 27, 2026: "Improving Wage Protections for the Temporary and Permanent Employment of Certain Foreign Nationals in the United States" (RIN 1205-AC30, 91 FR 15454). That one would change how the four prevailing wage levels are computed for PERM and for H-1B, H-1B1 and E-3 labor condition applications. Its comment period closed on May 26, 2026, and no final rule has been published. It's about wages; RIN 1205-AC29 is about the labor market test.

| | RIN 1205-AC29 | RIN 1205-AC30 |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Recruitment, layoffs, fair hiring, records | Prevailing wage levels |
| Proposed rule published | Not yet | March 27, 2026 |
| Comments | Not open yet | Closed May 26, 2026 |
| In force | No | No |

## What it means for a case today

Nothing changes until a final rule takes effect. A PERM filed now is judged under 20 CFR 656 as it reads today: a filing at least 30 days after the last mandatory recruitment step and within 180 days of the first (20 CFR 656.17(e)), the layoff look-back in 656.17(k), and the records kept under 656.10(f). Planning recruitment around a rule nobody has read yet would be planning around a guess. The attorney on the case is the person to ask how a final rule might touch a specific filing.
