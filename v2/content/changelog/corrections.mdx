This is the running log of everything this site has published that was wrong
about its data, its process or the rules. Entries are added, never removed and
never softened, and each one names the page, quotes what it said, states what
was true, and says what changed so it can't recur.

The bar for an entry is a reader who could have formed a false belief from the
page; an internal bug alone doesn't qualify. A wrong number on a live page
qualifies. A typo doesn't, and a design somebody disliked doesn't unless it misled.

## The privacy policy · Oct 29, 2026

**It said:** Google's Gemini, the AI chat's main provider, "Does not use API data to train models", and Mistral's API data "is not used for model training". Its list of how long each provider keeps a request said OpenRouter keeps nothing ("Not stored (Zero Data Retention)") and Gemini deletes requests after 55 days. The Terms of Service said the chat's providers "don't use your data to train AI models".

**What was true:** The chat runs on Gemini's unpaid quota, whose terms let Google use what's sent, and its answers, to improve its products, and let human reviewers read it. Mistral may use API requests to improve its services unless the account opts out, and OpenRouter passes each request to a model's own provider, whose policy decides.

**What changed:** The section says what each provider's own terms allow, and that a case's details go to them only when the chat reads that case to answer a question. The retention list and the Terms now say the same. [See the corrected section](/privacy#ai-chat-assistant).

## The employer switching panel · Oct 4, 2026

**It said:** "180 days after approval, the priority date moves with you."

**What was true:** Under 8 CFR 204.5(e) the priority date is kept from approval, unless USCIS revokes the petition for fraud, a revoked certification or a material error. The 180 days decide only whether withdrawing the petition revokes it.

## The deadline calculator, the case tracker and the deadline guides · Oct 16, 2026

**It said:** The ETA 9089 filing window always closes when the prevailing wage determination expires, if that comes before 180 days after the first recruitment step. The tracker wouldn't save a filing dated after the expiry, and closed a case automatically when the determination expired before the filing.

**What was true:** [20 CFR 656.40(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-656/subpart-D/section-656.40) asks employers to "file their applications or begin the recruitment period" while the determination is valid, and either is enough. Recruitment that begins on or after the determination date and on or before its expiry can be filed after the determination expires, inside the 180-day window. The Board of Alien Labor Certification Appeals read the rule this way en banc in *Karl Storz Endoscopy-America* (2011-PER-00040, December 1, 2011). The expiry still closes the window early when recruitment began before the determination was issued. The August 29 correction below fixed a calculator that ignored the expiry, and went too far the other way.

**What changed:** One rule in the code decides when the determination still limits a case, and the calculator, the case form, the deadline reminders, the automatic closing and the guides all read it. The calculator asks for the determination date, so it can tell which case you're in. [See the corrected calculator](/tools/perm-deadline-calculator).
