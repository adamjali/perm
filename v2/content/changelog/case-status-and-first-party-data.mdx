The site now answers the first question anyone waiting on a PERM case asks, and
every figure behind it comes from the agency that publishes it.

## Your own case, by number

[PERM case status](/perm-case-status) takes one PERM case number and returns
DOL's own status with a plain-English reading beside it, how many undecided
cases were filed ahead of it, and the sponsoring employer's published record.
Every deadline in the glossary carries the section of 20 CFR part 656 it comes
from, and the statuses DOL publishes no definition for say so rather than
getting one invented.

On a case that hasn't been decided, leave an email address and one message
follows when the status changes. It's double opt-in, it's offered only where a
case can still move, and nothing else is ever sent.

## The data comes from DOL directly

Per-case status used to be mirrored from a third party. It now comes from
DOL's own case-status search, in batches, run against every undecided case
twice a day and across the whole corpus daily. Checked field by field before
the switch, status, employer, job title and submitted date matched the old
mirror exactly, and DOL returns the visa type, which the mirror didn't.

That closes the last live third-party dependency on the site. Every dataset
here now has a primary source of record: DOL for case status, processing times
and the quarterly disclosure files, the State Department for the visa
bulletin, USCIS for I-140 counts and the I-485 inventory.

## Three new data pages

- **[RFI, audits and appeals](/perm-rfi-audit)**: what each review stage means,
  who has to act and by when, and a live count of how many cases are standing
  at each one. It publishes no audit rate, because our own measurement of that
  reads zero and a hole in the data isn't a finding.
- **[Decision activity](/perm-decision-activity)**: how many decisions DOL
  issues each day and week, the shape of its working week, and what happened to
  the cases it decided. It reports and forecasts nothing.
- **[Queue backlog](/perm-queue)**: how many cases are still undecided in every
  filing month and which DOL queue each sits in. DOL's quarterly files carry a
  decision date on every row and no pending rows at all, so this is underivable
  from them and comes from the per-case scan instead.

## 84 months of visa bulletin

The [priority date calculator](/tools/priority-date-calculator) now runs on 84
months of State Department bulletins, October 2019 through September 2026, all
six employment categories, with no third-party rows in it. Cutoff dates that
read C or U are drawn as what they are, one meaning the category was open to
every priority date and the other meaning it was shut to all of them, and a
closed run is drawn as a break in the line rather than interpolated through.

## Four stages, four calculators

[The calculators](/calculators) now cover each stage on its own terms: the
[prevailing wage queue](/tools/pwd-calculator), the
[PERM decision estimator](/tools/perm-timeline-calculator), the
[I-140 calculator](/tools/i140-calculator) and
[I-485 queue position](/tools/i485-queue-position), with the
[deadline calculator](/tools/perm-deadline-calculator) computing the dates the
regulations fix rather than estimating them.
