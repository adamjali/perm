Maplebear, Inc. d/b/a Instacart (Citizenship Status) October 2026

On October 19, 2026, the Immigrant and Employee Rights Section (IER) of the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division, in partnership with the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC), finalized a settlement agreement with Maplebear, Inc. d/b/a Instacart. The settlement resolves IER’s investigation which found that Instacart discriminated against U.S. workers when the company recruited as part of permanent labor certification (PERM) process. Specifically,  Instacart departed from its standard recruitment and required U.S. workers to mail applications when applying to PERM positions. In some instances, Instacart failed to timely consider U.S. workers because of this mail requirement. As part of the settlement, Instacart will create a settlement fund of up to $4,000,000 to compensate victims of the discrimination, to include both back pay and compensatory damages. Instacart will also pay $1,000,000 in civil penalties to the United States. The settlement also requires Instacart to give U.S. workers a fair opportunity to apply by posting PERM positions on its career website, allowing electronic applications, and entering candidates in its central applicant-tracking system. Instacart also will submit to training and monitoring requirements for three years.

Cloudera, Inc. (Citizenship Status) October 2026

On October 13, 2026, the Immigrant and Employee Rights Section (IER) of the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division, in partnership with the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL), finalized a settlement agreement with Cloudera, Inc. (Cloudera). The agreement resolves IER’s lawsuit alleging that, from at least March 31, 2024, until at least January 28, 2025, the company created a non-functional email address that made it impossible for some U.S. workers to apply for jobs it advertised in connection with permanent labor certifications, commonly referred to as the PERM process. The lawsuit further alleged that Cloudera discriminated against U.S. workers by deterring them from applying for jobs, and thus failed to consider them. Cloudera’s settlement with IER requires the company to pay $600,000 in civil penalties and up to $1.4 million for a back-pay fund to compensate victims of the discrimination. In addition, the settlement requires Cloudera to conduct all recruitment activities for PERM-related positions in the same way that it conducts recruitment for similar non-PERM-related positions, such as by hiring recruiters, posting on its career website, and entering candidates in its central applicant-tracking system. Cloudera will submit to IER’s training and monitoring requirements for three years. The agreement also resolves DOL’s suspension of Cloudera’s PERM certifications, including a two-year self-debarment period since its last PERM withdrawal and a commitment to join the U.S. Department of War’s SkillBridge program.

OpenAI (Citizenship Status) August 2026

On August 3, 2026, IER finalized a settlement agreement with OpenAI OpCo, LLC and Statsig, Inc. (together, OpenAI) to resolve findings arising from four charge-based investigations, and an independent investigation, that the companies engaged in citizenship status discrimination in hiring during permanent labor certification (PERM) recruitment, in violation of the Immigration and Nationality Act. IER’s investigations found that the companies did not allow U.S. workers to submit applications to PERM-related positions in the same manner they accept other job applications, such as electronically through their external job website. Instead, the companies required U.S. workers to submit their applications by mail. IER determined that this additional burden was not required under the PERM process and did not occur during non-PERM recruitment. The agreement requires OpenAI to pay $1.2 million in civil penalties and $2 million for a back-pay fund to compensate victims of the discrimination. In addition, OpenAI will undergo compliance monitoring for three years, which requires the company to train its employees and conduct more expansive recruitment for PERM positions. These changes include posting PERM positions on external job websites, accepting electronic applications, and entering people who applied for PERM positions into OpenAI’s applicant tracking system.
