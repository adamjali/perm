Annual Report of Immigrant Visa Applicants in the Family-sponsored and 
Employment-based preferences Registered at the National Visa Center 
as of November 1, 2023 
  
Most prospective immigrant visa applicants qualify for immigrant status under U.S. law on the basis of 
family relationships or employer sponsorship. Entitlement to visa processing in these classes is 
established ordinarily through U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) approval of a petition 
filed on the applicant's behalf by the sponsoring family member or employer. USCIS forwards the 
approved petitions for applicants who will be processed at an overseas U.S. embassy or consulate to the 
Department of State. Applicants in categories subject under law to a numerical limit are registered on 
the visa waiting list discussed below.  Note that applications for adjustment of status under U.S. law 
(Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) Section 245) which are pending at USCIS Offices are not included 
in the tabulation of the immigrant waiting list data provided below. As such, the following figures ONLY 
reflect approved petitions which the Department of State has received, and do not include the 
significant number of applications held with the USCIS Offices.  
Each case is assigned a priority date based on the filing date accorded to the petition; this is the 
applicant’s place in line.  When their priority date is within the dates for filing published each month in 
the Department of State’s Visa Bulletin, the Department of State’s National Visa Center (NVC) advises 
the applicant to pay fees and assemble and submit documents required for visa processing (i.e., become 
documentarily complete).  Visa issuance within each numerically limited category is possible only when 
the applicant's priority date is within the applicable final action dates, also published in the Visa Bulletin.  
Eligible family-based and employment-based immigrant visa applicants are subject to overall worldwide 
annual numerical limits within their respective categories; in addition, INA 202 sets a per-country 
maximum on the number of family and employment-based visas which may be issued in a single year to 
applicants from any one country regardless of where they are processed.  The totals in this report reflect 
applicants with approved petitions slated for overseas visa application at all stages of this process – 
those that are not yet documentarily complete, those that are documentarily complete and waiting for 
their priority date to become current, and those whose priority date is current and are waiting to be 
scheduled for an interview. 
The following figures are compiled from the NVC report submitted to the Department on November 1, 
2023, and show the number of immigrant visa applicants on the visa waiting list in the various 
preferences and subcategories subject to numerical limit. All figures reflect persons registered under 
each numerical limitation, i.e., the totals represent not only principal applicants or petition beneficiaries, 
but their spouses and children entitled to derivative status under INA Section 203(d) as well.  
The Employment-based totals provided in this year’s waiting list report are considerably higher than last 
year.  This is in part due to the Employment-based annual limit, calculated based on the procedures 
established in INA Section 201(d), returning to baseline levels after record highs in the immediate post-
pandemic years.  Cases not processed under these lower annual limits have been retained at NVC and 
included in this year’s report.  Please see here for reporting on the interview-ready Immigrant Visa 
Scheduling Backlog, a subset of this total data.

Immigrant Waiting List 
By Country 
  
Immigrant visa issuances during fiscal year 2024 will be limited by the terms of INA Section 201 
to no more than 226,000 in the family-sponsored preferences and approximately 161,000 in 
the employment-based preferences. (Visas for "Immediate Relatives" - i.e., spouses, unmarried 
children under the age of 21 years, and parents of U.S. citizens - are not subject to numerical 
limitation.)  
  
It should by no means be assumed that once an applicant is registered, the case is continually 
included in the waiting list totals unless and until a visa is issued. Consular procedures mandate 
a regular culling of visa cases to remove from the count those unlikely to see further action, so 
that totals are not unreasonably inflated.  
 
The ten countries with the highest number of waiting list registrants for both family and 
employment visas in FY 2024 are listed below; together these represent 74.9% of the total. This 
list includes all countries with at least 78,000 persons on the waiting list. INA Section 202 
prescribes that visa issuances to nationals of any single country may not exceed seven percent 
during a fiscal year. This limit serves to avoid the potential monopolization of the annual 
limitation by applicants from only a few countries. That limitation is not a quota to which any 
particular country is entitled, however. For FY 2024 the per-country limit will be approximately 
27,090.

Family SECOND Preference:  
  
The total Family SECOND preference waiting list figure is 723,622. Of these, 337,958 (46.7%) are 
spouses and children of permanent residents of the United States (the 2A class), and 385,664 
(53.3%) are adult unmarried sons/daughters of permanent residents (the 2B class). The Family 
SECOND preference represents 19.2% of the total Family preference waiting list. It will receive 
114,200 visa numbers for FY 2024, just over half of the 226,000 family preference total; 77% of 
SECOND preference numbers are provided to 2A applicants, while the remaining 23% go to the 
2B class.

increase and partially offset new F2A filings.

Employment-based Immigrant Waiting List by Country 
  
It is important to note that normally about eighty-five percent of all Employment preference 
immigrants are processed as adjustment of status cases at USCIS offices. Cases pending with 
USCIS are not counted in the consular waiting list tally presented below. Therefore, in several 
Employment categories the waiting list totals being provided below significantly understate 
true immigrant demand. The Employment waiting list counts not only prospective workers, but 
also their spouses and children entitled under the law to derivative preference status.

least 9,500 persons on the waiting list. (INA 202 sets an annual maximum per country limit on

The Fifth Preference (C5, I5, R5, and T5) categories are “current” at present for all countries 
except China and India. The EB-5 Reform and Integrity Act of 2022 reserved Employment Fifth 
Preference visas for qualified immigrants who invest in rural areas, high unemployment areas, 
and infrastructure projects.  These set-aside categories are “current” at present for all 
countries.  State and USCIS anticipate issuing these visas in the coming fiscal year, and final 
action dates may be set if demand for any of these categories exceeds the annual limits.
